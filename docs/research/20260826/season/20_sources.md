L592: 
L593: As summer transitions to autumn, Japan begins to cool down, offering temperate weather that's perfect for outdoor walks. From mid-November to early December, the trees turn vivid hues of red, orange, and yellow. It's this combination of cool weather and striking sights that make fall a great season for traveling to Japan.

L603: 
L604:   * During autumn, humidity drops and temperatures begin to fall. Though the weather is often pleasant, bring a few extra layers for the cooler evenings
L605:   * Many Japanese have holidays in late September. Consider timing your trip to avoid crowds at their peak

L607: ### Bold autumn colors and seasonal flower blooms
L608: 
L609: As the summer heat wanes and a winter chill begins to set in, Japan's dense forests begin turning hues of vivid orange, yellow, and red. Traditional gardens and parks showcase these autumn colors and attract crowds of visitors eager to see the changing leaves.
L610: 
L611: If in Tokyo, be sure to head to the cite326†Rikugien Garden cite317†Image†res.cloudinary.com cite318†Image†res.cloudinary.com , a traditional Japanese garden with striking autumn views.

L620: 
L621: The changing colors and temperate weather make autumn an ideal time for traditional festivals and events. Regardless of your location, you're sure to encounter numerous local festivities, each boasting centuries of rich history and traditions.
L622: One famous festival during this season is the cite333†Nagasaki Kunchi Festival cite317†Image†res.cloudinary.com cite318†Image†res.cloudinary.com . Celebrating the autumn harvest, the festival is said to have started in 1634. Held in the beginning of October, the festival also features a unique connection with the west, given the presence of Dutch traders off the coast of cite334†Nagasaki cite317†Image†res.cloudinary.com cite318†Image†res.cloudinary.com in the 17th century.
L623: You'll witness processions of mikoshi floats, Chinese-influenced dragon dances, and Dutch-inspired rituals.
L624: Later in October, the residents of Kyoto celebrate the cite335†Jidai Matsuri cite317†Image†res.cloudinary.com cite318†Image†res.cloudinary.com , which is known as one of city's significant festivals. The festival started to commemorate the founding of Heian Jingu Shrine, and the 1,100th anniversary of the relocation of the capital to Heian (present-day Kyoto), and features a long procession of portable mikoshi shrines, samurai-clad locals, and traditional music performances.
L625: The parade ends at cite336†Heian Jingu Shrine cite317†Image†res.cloudinary.com cite318†Image†res.cloudinary.com where the revelry continues.
L626: Most other regions and cities have their own noteworthy festivals, such as the Autumn Ginza event in Tokyo and the Nihonmatsu Lantern Festival in Fukushima. These festivals provide a glimpse into Japan's rich history and long-lasting traditions.

L632: 
L633: The perfect weather and low rainfall make fall a prime time for sporting events and outdoor recreation.
